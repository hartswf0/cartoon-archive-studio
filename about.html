Visual Essay & System Explainer // 345 Categories × 270 Full-Frame SCION Multiplane Cartoons

How to Turn 50 Million Human Doodles & Archival Film into a Living Multiplane Cartoon Archive

Google’s Quick, Draw! dataset captures how human beings mentally compress the visual world into 5 to 15 rapid pen strokes—an extraordinary dictionary of iconic form across 345 categories, but frozen in static 2D sketches. Conversely, the Internet Archive’s Moving Image Archive preserves a century of real physical weight, locomotion, fluid dynamics, and camera choreography—but trapped inside noisy historical pixels.

Inspired by Jordan Mechner’s 1989 Prince of Persia rotoscoping workflow, Disney’s 1937 Multiplane Camera, and the Secondhand Motion benchmark, this project builds a SCION-FullFrame-v8 Multiplane Engine + Salient Secondary-Region Redraw & Locally Color-Sampled Gouache Cels that evolves minimal human doodles into full 7-plane animated cartoons across 270 shots (`8,910` frames) and 8 radically distinct kinematic and ontological regimes.

THE CENTRAL PRINCIPLE // CLASSICAL ANIMATION LOOP DRAW → LOOK → COMPARE → ADJUST
Primitive Operation:
Archival Film (33f) + Quick, Draw! Seed → Auto-DINO/Depth/SAM Silhouette → 4 Keyframe Doodle Drawings → Skeleton Centerline Trace → 4-Episode Argon RL Policy Evolution → 7-Plane Multiplane Camera

Why Naive Approaches Fail (The Cartoon Archive Trilemma)

  • Failure Mode 1 — Frame-by-Frame Image Diffusion (Boiling): Redrawing each video frame independently with an image generator causes violent background boil, vanishing strokes, and loss of the original Quick, Draw! stroke topology.
  • Failure Mode 2 — Rigid/Affine Warping of One Doodle (Flatness): Translating or stretching a single static 2D doodle across a video cannot articulate scissoring legs, rotating wheels, tilting lighthouse towers, or opening butterfly wings.
  • Failure Mode 3 — Pixel-Wise Gradient Crumpling (Squiggles): Pushing individual stroke vertices down the pixel gradient of a distance transform folds and crumples strokes that cross mask boundaries (such as a canoeist’s paddle or an elephant’s tusk).

The 7-Stage Universal Zero-Hardcoding Architecture

STAGE 01 // ARCHIVE

33-Frame Cut & 4 Key Poses

Sample 33 continuous frames (f000..f032 @ 12fps) and extract 4 chronological reference keyframes (K01..K04 at f00, f10, f21, f32).

STAGE 02 // AUTO-SAM

DINO + Depth + Flow + SAM

STAGE 03 // LOOK LANE

4 Doodle Keys + Scene Plate

Select a recognized Quick, Draw! seed (design.png), draw 4 chronological character keyframes on pure white (selected-keys.png), and draw a unique scene-plate.png.

STAGE 04 // VECTORIZE

Morphological Centerline Trace

STAGE 05 // ARGON RL

4-Episode Overlay Evolution

Evolve from raw doodle (Ep 0) to Box Registration (Ep 1), 2D Smooth Cage (Ep 2), and Rotoscope-Fused Keyframe Policy (Ep 3).

STAGE 06 // DEPTH STACK

7-Plane Stratification

Partition scene strokes using real Depth-Anything-V2 depth maps across 7 multiplane sheets (z=10.0 Sky down to z=0.85 Foreground Mask).

STAGE 07 // CAMERA

Multiplane Composite

Photograph all 7 layers through a virtual multiplane camera with Pass A/B construction lines, watertight cel occlusion, and Foley soundtrack.

Section 03 // Mathematical Formulation & Diagnostic Overlay

How the Argon RL Policy Evaluates & Evolves Drawings Against SAM

Zero Per-Shot Hardcoding
1. Auto-Synthesized SAM Prompts

Viterbi Trajectory Linker + Medial-Axis SAM Prompts

Instead of hand-tuning bounding boxes per video, our engine fuses GroundingDINO text detections with Depth-Anything-V2 foreground depth, Farnebäck optical flow, and a Viterbi Dynamic Programming Trajectory Linker.

On every frame, it computes foreground salience inside the Viterbi-smoothed box to place Positive Points along internal structural ridges and Negative Points in background pockets, plus a Waterline Filter that prevents water reflection bleed.

2. Color-Coded Match/Fail Overlay

Reading the Live Diagnostic Overlay

To see exactly where a doodle matches or fails the archival motion, the engine renders a 4-column diagnostic overlay across all keyframes:

  • BRIGHT GREEN INK & FILL: Cartoon strokes and enclosed character cel that accurately lock onto the SAM boundary and interior silhouette.
  • RED SILHOUETTE & CONTOUR: Archival SAM silhouette regions and boundary edges missed/uncovered by the current drawing.
  • BLUE STROKES: Cartoon strokes that drifted outside the dilated SAM mask.
3. 4-Episode Smooth 2D Cage Policy

Reward Function & Elastic Regularization

For each frame, the Argon RL policy evaluates candidate smooth 2D cage + radial boundary warps to maximize:

R(S, M) = 0.35 · IoU_sil(Cel(S), M)
        + 0.35 · Recall_edge(S, ∂M)
        + 0.30 · Precision_in(S, M)
        − λ ||ΔC||²

Because the deformation acts on a regularized smooth $2\times 2$ bilinear cage and 16-ray angular envelope rather than raw pixel gradients, strokes never crumple or fold.

Section 05 // Empirical Findings Across Diverse Cases

What Testing Across 70 Cartoons & 8 Radically Different Regimes Reveals

70 Shots // Predators, Ungulates, Marine, Vehicles, Aerospace, Music, Architecture & Nature

A pipeline that only animates side-profile walking quadrupeds or translating cars can hide severe architectural blind spots. By pushing the exact same zero-hardcoding engine across 70 shots spanning 8 distinct ontological and kinematic regimes from the 345-category archive, we uncover how each regime stresses segmentation, topology, and multiplane depth:

Regime I // Terrestrial Locomotion (18 Shots) giraffe, dog, horse, lion, tiger, bear, zebra, camel, kangaroo...

Articulated Limbs, Scissoring Gaits & World-Space Foot Lock

What it tests: Non-rigid limb articulation where legs cross over one another (`giraffe`, `horse`, `zebra`), predators prowl (`lion`, `tiger`, `bear`), bipedal hoppers bound (`rabbit`, `kangaroo`), primates climb (`monkey`, `panda`, `raccoon`), or megafauna swing trunks (`elephant`).
Key finding: Generating 4 chronological keyframe poses on white ground + anchoring contact baselines in world space prevents foot sliding while preserving open hand-drawn contour gaps via watertight SAM cel sealing.

Regime II // Wheeled & Rail Machines (10 Shots) bicycle, tractor, train, car, bus, firetruck, ambulance, motorbike, bulldozer, roller-coaster

Perspective Foreshortening & Internal Structural Grids

What it tests: Vehicles advancing diagonally or head-on toward the lens (`tractor`, `train`, `firetruck`, `bulldozer`, `roller-coaster`), where scale changes over 33 frames and internal geometry (cab windows, ladders, radiator grilles, spoked wheels) must not shear.
Key finding: Constraining Episode 3 to regularized smooth $2\times 2$ cage registration + rotoscope interior contours preserves crisp cab windows and wheel circles across perspective looms.

Regime III // Nautical, Marine & Waterline (14 Shots) sailboat, canoe, speedboat, duck, swan, penguin, flamingo, shark, whale, dolphin, sea-turtle, crab, aircraft-carrier, cruise-ship

Waterline Occlusion & Dark Water-Reflection Filtering

What it tests: Buoyant hulls (`sailboat`, `canoe`, `speedboat`, `aircraft-carrier`, `cruise-ship`), waterfowl (`duck`, `swan`, `penguin`, `flamingo`), and marine swimmers (`shark`, `whale`, `dolphin`, `sea-turtle`, `crab`) riding or breaching specular water.
Key finding: Our Universal Waterline Filter + bottom-border negative SAM points prevent masks from leaking into dark water reflections beneath the subject, while Plane 3 (`secondary`) animates synchronized water wake ripples.

Regime IV // Rotary, Avian & Aerial Flight (8 Shots) helicopter, windmill, hot-air-balloon, airplane, parachute, flying-saucer, owl, parrot

Thin Swept Blades, Buoyant Envelopes & Sky Parallax

What it tests: Thin lattice sails (`windmill`), rotorcraft & fixed-wing aircraft (`helicopter`, `airplane`), descending canopies (`parachute`), discs (`flying-saucer`), avian perches (`owl`, `parrot`), and ascending ribbed aerostats (`hot-air-balloon`).
Key finding: Separating the primary aerial actor (`z=2.0`) from distant cloud/horizon planes (`z=10..3.5`) creates genuine multiplane flight parallax.

Regime V // Amorphous Natural Phenomena (3 Shots) campfire, tornado, ocean

Stochastic Flickering Flames, Vortex Funnels & Breaking Surf

What it tests: Elemental forces (`campfire` flames, `tornado` funnel cloud, breaking `ocean` surf) whose boundaries deform continuously rather than following rigid skeletal bones.
Key finding: The 4-keyframe rotoscope-doodle representation captures discrete wave/flame/vortex states (`K01..K04`), while the smooth 2D cage and 16-ray radial warp track the evolving optical-flow/SAM envelope.

Regime VI // Musical Performance (5 Shots) guitar, piano, violin, cello, trumpet

Human-Instrument Interaction & Fine Internal Landmarks

What it tests: Acoustic and orchestral instruments (`guitar`, `piano`, `violin`, `cello`, `trumpet`) played in performance—featuring parallel strings, keyboards, f-holes, brass valves, and bowing motion.
Key finding: Morphological centerline thinning (`morph_skeletonize`) preserves fine strings, keys, and soundholes as crisp strokes (`recall > 0.99`), while stage environments live on background planes.

Regime VII // Everyday Mechanisms & Optics (2 Shots) clock, camera

Close-Up Concentric Dial/Lens Geometry & Tabletop Parallax

What it tests: Macro shots of precision mechanical objects (`clock`, `camera`) where any high-frequency warp distorts circular bezels, lens barrels, or dial numerals.
Key finding: Regularized $2\times 2$ cage registration translates and scales precision mechanisms smoothly across the frame without warping concentric circular dials or lenses.

Regime VIII // Architecture, Invertebrates & Flora (10 Shots) lighthouse, bridge, castle, church, barn, butterfly, frog, snail, spider, flower

Monumental Landmarks, Camera Tilts & Delicate Biological Motion

What it tests: Monumental architecture under camera pan/tilt (`lighthouse`, `bridge`, `castle`, `church`, `barn`) alongside delicate biological articulation (`butterfly`, `frog`, `snail`, `spider`, blooming `flower`).
Key finding: Multiplane depth stratification (`Planes 6..0`) separates foreground framing foliage and bridge piers (`z=1.25..0.85`) from the primary architectural or biological subject (`z=2.0`).
