Visual Essay & System Explainer // 345 Categories × 20 Production Cartoons

How to Turn 50 Million Human Doodles & Archival Film into a Living Multiplane Cartoon Archive

Google’s Quick, Draw! dataset captures how human beings mentally compress the visual world into 5 to 15 rapid pen strokes—an extraordinary dictionary of iconic form across 345 categories, but frozen in static 2D sketches. Conversely, the Internet Archive’s Moving Image Archive preserves a century of real physical weight, locomotion, fluid dynamics, and camera choreography—but trapped inside noisy historical pixels.

Inspired by Jordan Mechner’s 1989 Prince of Persia rotoscoping workflow, Disney’s 1937 Multiplane Camera, and the Secondhand Motion benchmark, this project builds a Universal Zero-Hardcoding Argon RL Multiplane Engine that evolves minimal human doodles into full 7-plane animated cartoons across 8 radically distinct kinematic and ontological regimes.

THE CENTRAL PRINCIPLE // CLASSICAL ANIMATION LOOP DRAW → LOOK → COMPARE → ADJUST
Primitive Operation:
Archival Film (33f) + Quick, Draw! Seed → Auto-DINO/Depth/SAM Silhouette → 4 Keyframe Doodle Drawings → Skeleton Centerline Trace → 4-Episode Argon RL Policy Evolution → 7-Plane Multiplane Camera

Why Naive Approaches Fail (The Cartoon Archive Trilemma)

  • Failure Mode 1 — Frame-by-Frame Image Diffusion (Boiling): Redrawing each video frame independently with an image generator causes violent background boil, vanishing strokes, and loss of the original Quick, Draw! stroke topology.
  • Failure Mode 2 — Rigid/Affine Warping of One Doodle (Flatness): Translating or stretching a single static 2D doodle across a video cannot articulate scissoring legs, rotating wheels, tilting lighthouse towers, or opening butterfly wings.
  • Failure Mode 3 — Pixel-Wise Gradient Crumpling (Squiggles): Pushing individual stroke vertices down the pixel gradient of a distance transform folds and crumples strokes that cross mask boundaries (such as a canoeist’s paddle or an elephant’s tusk).

STAGE 06 // DEPTH STACK

7-Plane Stratification

STAGE 07 // CAMERA

Multiplane Composite

Photograph all 7 layers through a virtual multiplane camera with Pass A/B construction lines, watertight cel occlusion, and Foley soundtrack.

Section 03 // Mathematical Formulation & Diagnostic Overlay

How the Argon RL Policy Evaluates & Evolves Drawings Against SAM

Zero Per-Shot Hardcoding
1. Auto-Synthesized SAM Prompts

Medial-Axis Positive + Pocket Negative Points

Instead of hand-tuning bounding boxes per video, universal_auto_sam_pass fuses GroundingDINO text detections with Depth-Anything-V2 foreground depth, Farnebäck optical flow, and GrabCut color contrast.

On every frame, it computes the L2 distance transform of the prior mask to place 6 Positive Points (Cyan) along the internal skeleton ridge and 6 Negative Points (Red) in low-probability background pockets, plus a Temporal Waterline Filter that trims narrow bottom reflection spikes.

2. Color-Coded Match/Fail Overlay

Reading the Live Diagnostic Overlay

To see exactly where a doodle matches or fails the archival motion, the engine renders a 4-column diagnostic overlay across all keyframes:

  • BRIGHT GREEN INK & FILL: Cartoon strokes and enclosed character cel that accurately lock onto the SAM boundary and interior silhouette.
  • RED SILHOUETTE & CONTOUR: Archival SAM silhouette regions and boundary edges missed/uncovered by the current drawing.
  • BLUE STROKES: Cartoon strokes that drifted outside the dilated SAM mask.
3. 4-Episode Smooth 2D Cage Policy

Reward Function & Elastic Regularization

For each frame, the Argon RL policy evaluates candidate smooth 2D row/column cage warps $\mathbf{a} = (\alpha_{\text{row}}, \alpha_{\text{col}}, \delta_{\text{inset}})$ to maximize:

R(S, M) = 0.40 · IoU_sil(Cel(S), M)
        + 0.35 · Recall_edge(S, ∂M)
        + 0.25 · Precision_in(S, M)
        − λ(α_row² + α_col²)

Because the deformation acts on smooth 14-band Gaussian-filtered row/column spans rather than raw pixel gradients, strokes never crumple or fold.

Section 05 // Empirical Findings Across Diverse Cases

What Testing Across 8 Radically Different Regimes Reveals

20 Shots // Animals, Vehicles, Fire, Music, Clocks, Architecture, Flight & Flora/Insects

A pipeline that only animates side-profile walking quadrupeds or translating cars can hide severe architectural blind spots. By pushing the exact same zero-hardcoding engine across 8 distinct ontological and kinematic regimes from the 345-category archive, we uncover how each regime stresses segmentation, topology, and multiplane depth:

Regime I // Terrestrial Locomotion giraffe, dog, horse, rabbit, elephant

Articulated Limbs, Scissoring Gaits & World-Space Foot Lock

What it tests: Non-rigid limb articulation where legs cross over one another (`giraffe`, `horse`), posture shifts from frontal to profile (`rabbit`), or massive self-occluding trunks and tusks swing (`elephant`).
Key finding: A single warped doodle fails because topology changes across the gait cycle. Generating 4 chronological keyframe poses on white ground + anchoring contact baselines in world space prevents foot sliding while preserving open hand-drawn contour gaps via morphological hull-bounded cel sealing.

Regime II // Wheeled & Rail Machines bicycle, tractor, train

Perspective Foreshortening & Internal Structural Grids

What it tests: Vehicles advancing diagonally or head-on toward the lens (`tractor`, `train` locomotive #53), where scale doubles over 33 frames and internal geometry (4 cab windows, radiator grille, spoked wheels) must not shear.
Key finding: High-frequency pixel-gradient warps destroy straight mechanical lines and round wheels. Constraining Episode 3 to low-frequency 2D cage registration (`col_blend ≤ 0.12`) preserves crisp cab windows and wheel circles across 2× scale looms (`train SilIoU = 0.95`).

Regime III // Nautical & Waterline Craft sailboat, canoe, speedboat, duck

Waterline Occlusion & Dark Water-Reflection Filtering

What it tests: Buoyant hulls and waterfowl riding a specular fluid surface where dark upside-down reflections (`duck`) or submerged paddle blades (`canoe`) fool standard segmentation.
Key finding: Without temporal aspect-ratio regularization, SAM masks periodically leak into dark water reflections beneath the subject, vertically stretching the character. Our Universal Temporal Waterline Filter detects narrow bottom appendages (`row_w < 0.50 · peak_w`) that exceed the clip’s 20th-percentile height-to-width baseline and slices them cleanly at the waterline.

Regime IV // Rotary & Aerial Machines helicopter, windmill, hot-air-balloon

Thin Swept Blades, Buoyant Envelopes & Sky Parallax

What it tests: Thin lattice sails (`windmill`), banking rotorcraft (`helicopter`), and ascending ribbed envelopes with suspended baskets (`hot-air-balloon`) surrounded by competing sky objects.
Key finding: Separating the primary ascending balloon (`z=2.0`) from companion festival balloons placed on distant multiplane sheets (`z=6.0..3.5`) creates genuine aerial depth parallax without merging adjacent envelopes.

Regime V // Amorphous Natural Phenomena campfire

Stochastic Flickering Flames Anchored to Rigid Fuel Logs

What it tests: Fire is neither a rigid vehicle nor a skeletal animal—its upper boundary flickers stochastically while its lower wooden teepee logs remain stationary on the riverbank.
Key finding: The 4-keyframe doodle representation naturally captures discrete flame tongue states (`K01..K04`), while the smooth 2D cage keeps the bottom log pyramid planted on the rocky clearing (`z=2.0`) and lets the upper flame tips dance with the optical-flow/SAM envelope.

Regime VI // Musical Performance guitar

Human-Instrument Interaction & Fine Internal Landmarks

What it tests: An acoustic guitar held by a seated performer—featuring concentric soundhole rings, parallel strings, fret markers, and a figure-eight bouts contour swaying in rhythm.
Key finding: Morphological centerline thinning (`morph_skeletonize`) preserves the parallel guitar strings and circular soundhole as single crisp strokes (`recall > 0.99`), while the performer’s shoes, mic cable, and stage rug live on background/midground planes.

Regime VII // Everyday Tabletop Mechanisms clock

Close-Up Concentric Dial Typography & Tabletop Parallax

What it tests: A macro tabletop shot of a vintage mechanical alarm clock flanked by patterned porcelain canisters, where any radial distortion immediately warps the circular bezel and numerals (`12, 3, 6, 9`).
Key finding: High-contrast GrabCut + Depth-Anything-V2 cleanly separates the foreground clock dial from the floral kitchen canisters behind it, and low-distortion Ep 3 registration translates the clock smoothly across the counter without warping the dial circle.

Regime VIII // Architecture & Winged Biology lighthouse, butterfly

Vertical Camera Tilts & Bilateral Wing Articulation

What it tests: Extreme vertical camera tilt from the base cottage to the top lantern cupola of a striped `lighthouse`, vs. stationary-body bilateral wing flapping of a `butterfly` perched on garden blossoms.
Key finding: In `lighthouse`, the 4 keyframes progress structurally up the architectural shaft (`base bands → middle bands → gallery rim → domed beacon`), while framing trees sit on foreground occlusion planes (`z=1.25`). In `butterfly`, the 2D cage tracks the expanding/contracting wing span while the flower cluster remains rock-steady on its perch plane.
